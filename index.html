Preamble

Welcome to The Phoenix Coalition. Our mission is to build a better future through innovative policies and governance. The Phoenix Coalition believes that lasting freedom comes not from constant upheaval, but from rebuilding with discipline, fairness, and purpose. After prolonged polarization, economic inequality, and loss of trust in leadership, we refuse to choose between disorder and complacency. Instead, we rise from the ashes of past failures to forge a new path forward. We will lead with strength, enforcing law with consistency, protecting the economy, and respecting every citizen's right to live freely in their personal life. We believe in a government that is firm yet fair, one that upholds the rule of law while ensuring justice and opportunity for all while staying out of personal choices. The role of the government is to keep order, ensure opportunity, and defend liberty; not to moralize or divide. We will fight corruption wherever it lives, whether in government offices or boardrooms. We will rebuild trust in our institutions, restore dignity to work, and make sure that safety and stability are rights, not privileges.

Criminal Justice

The current criminal justice system in the United States is a failing institution that delivers neither safety to citizens nor fairness to its subjects. Two decades into reform, our nation still imprisons more than 2.2 million people, a number inflated by the nearly 10 million admissions processed through county jails every year. Violence has been in decline since the 1990s, but the Bureau of Justice Statistics estimates that almost two-thirds of released prisoners are rearrested within three years (“Incarceration & Reentry | ASPE”). This is evidence of a system that punishes efficiently yet rehabilitates poorly. The imbalance shows that the pursuit of justice has been supplanted by the machinery of bureaucracy, vast in scale and limited in effectiveness.

All elements of national stability suffer when a justice system goes awry. Where crime is endemic and enforcement lacks credibility, citizens lose faith in the institutions meant to keep them safe. Researchers estimate that about 40 percent of the incarcerated population is behind bars without a clear justification from a public safety standpoint, costing taxpayers billions without demonstrable evidence of deterrence. The fragmented and slow reporting of national crime data, identified by the Council on Criminal Justice as a key barrier to sound policymaking, prevents timely and targeted responses. A rudderless system diminishes both safety and liberty, undermining civic trust at its foundation. The legitimacy of the rule of law depends not on the fervor with which it punishes but on its precision.

The Phoenix Coalition believes liberty requires order and order requires accountability. Justice should be predictable, quantifiable, transparent, strict in defending peace yet restrained in intrusion into private life. The Phoenix Coalition rejects, as dangerous to freedom, both leniency that enables disorder and excess that erodes freedom. The purpose of law is not vengeance. It's equilibrium. A society cannot thrive when it tolerates lawlessness or punishes its own people as an industry. The party supports firm enforcement guided by data, fairness, and public oversight, ensuring that discipline strengthens liberty rather than restricts it.

The Phoenix Coalition advocates for a system based on truth, proportionality, and effectiveness. The cornerstone will be reliable data of national crime and sentencing through an unprecedented digital transparency effort that thoroughly tracks sentencing trends, recidivism rates, and enforcement outcomes. Evidence-based policing and judicial policy replace reactive enforcement, focusing resources on violent and repeat offenders rather than low-level, nonviolent cases. Targeted intervention has always been proven to cut violence far more effectively than mass incarceration. Sentencing reform will be based on proportionality and purpose, with mandatory minimums and outdated "three-strikes" laws revised to reflect actual public safety outcomes. With 17 percent of state prisoners incarcerated for nonviolent drug offenses, the need for judicial discretion and rehabilitative alternatives is clear. Evidence-based reentry programs focused on employment, housing stability, and behavioral accountability will be expanded. Research from the National Institute of Justice shows that such programs can reduce recidivism by up to 20 percent. Finally, transparency will be institutionalized through standardized public reporting of arrest, conviction, and sentencing data. Open access to performance metrics at every stage will ensure oversight, build public confidence, and prevent abuses of power.

Healthcare & Pharmaceutical Regulation

For too long, healthcare in the United States has been treated as a commodity rather than a collective necessity. The Phoenix Coalition rejects this notion. Health is not a product to be bought or sold; it is a tool of national survival. A nation’s longevity and strength depend on the physical and mental endurance of its people. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), expanding access to healthcare saves more than 212,000 lives in the United States every year. The average American life expectancy stands at approximately 77 years, compared with 68 years in countries with minimal healthcare infrastructure, such as India. These differences illustrate a fundamental truth: access to medical care determines whether a population thrives or decays.

A healthy population is a productive population. The Coalition views healthcare not as a matter of convenience, but as a foundation of national security and efficiency. Research from the Kaiser Family Foundation (2024) estimates that preventable illness costs the U.S. economy more than $260 billion annually in lost productivity. Illness undermines labor strength, weakens morale, and erodes the resilience of the workforce. Therefore, ensuring universal access to medical care is not merely a moral imperative — it is a strategic necessity. The the right to health carries with it a civic duty to maintain that health for the collective good.

The Phoenix Coalition guarantees universal medical care as a right of citizenship and a requirement of service. Every citizen, regardless of class or occupation, shall receive the treatment necessary to preserve their ability to perform their societal function. In return, all citizens must comply with regular health evaluations and contribute anonymized data to federal health systems for research and monitoring. Refusal to participate constitutes refusal to serve. Health resources are allocated by strategic priority: individuals essential to defense, production, or research receive priority in treatment and recovery. Medicine is distributed according to necessity, not desire. Rehabilitation is reserved for those who contribute to the collective; idleness does not merit restoration.

The Coalition envisions a healthcare system based on universal access, scientific regulation, and moral discipline. All pharmaceuticals and medical substances shall be subject to the oversight of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Each drug must pass through the FDA’s 12-step approval process, a safeguard established by the 1938 Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act to prevent the circulation of unsafe products. The unregulated creation or distribution of drugs constitutes a direct act against the nation’s welfare. Medication exists to heal, not to gratify.

Pharmaceutical production will occur exclusively under federal supervision. Government laboratories will control dosage, availability, and purpose of all medical products. This regulation will eliminate the epidemic of overdose that claims more than 110,000 American lives annually, according to the National Institute on Drug Abuse (2024). Recreational drug use is prohibited; chemical dependency weakens individual discipline and collective strength. The 6.8 million Americans who currently use marijuana recreationally represent the erosion of civic resilience. Scientific research links such use to delayed neurological development and dependency among adolescents. The Coalition stands unequivocally against these indulgences.

Scientific advancement remains a national priority. As of June 2025, the United States hosts roughly 159,000 registered clinical trials, with new drug development requiring an average of 10 to 12 years and $2.6 billion to complete. Only 12 percent of candidates achieve approval. These figures justify the Coalition’s centralized model of research oversight, ensuring that time, funding, and innovation are directed toward medicine that enhances survival and operational strength; trauma care, radiation treatment, and resistance medicine. Experimental treatments must be sanctioned through federal review to ensure safety and alignment with national objectives. The Coalition rejects medical decadence in all forms. Recreational pharmaceuticals and pain-numbing indulgences divert resources from genuine healing. Globally, 15.8 million aesthetic surgeries were performed in 2023, including 1.6 million in the United States, often with medical complications. In the same year, 14.2 million Americans misused prescription psychotherapeutic drugs, and 8.5 million abused pain relievers. Such misuse represents moral and civic decay. The Coalition affirms that medicine exists to restore function, not to gratify vanity. Within this system, the body serves the mission, medicine serves the people, and the people serve the nation.

No life will be wasted to negligence, indulgence, or inequity. Health is not a privilege; it is the instrument by which the Coalition ensures survival, progress, and the unbreakable vitality of the United States.

Education

The United States emerges from war with an education system that is fractured from decades of inaccessibility, deprivation, and neglect. Under the Capitol, learning was not a right but a privilege granted selectively. It was weaponized to maintain control and was stripped from many districts entirely; approximately 21% of U.S. adults are unable to complete literacy tasks at a level necessary for the modern workforce, and an estimated 130 million adults have low literacy skills. The result of inconsistent education is a nation whose citizens lack a solid academic foundation, a vocational skill set, and an understanding of civics. This divided education system has led to a divided people, and division cannot be afforded in the post-war nation.

The Coalition believes a stable nation cannot exist without an educated society. National resilience is built on the backbone of literacy, critical thinking, and practical competence. Without these skills, misinformation spreads easily and productively weakens, which slows our recovery from the war. The Phoenix Coalition understands that the same forces that enabled the dominant to dominate the nation, dependency and a lack of education, threaten long-term stability. Once the curriculum is predictable, accessible, and measurable, liberty and order will be strengthened.

The Phoenix Coalition asserts that education is a collective obligation: citizens have the duty to learn, while the nation has the responsibility to teach our generation. Our party proposes a national curriculum reform, which would have a unified national standard of education that must be taught nationwide, with emphasis on factual literacy, scientific knowledge, and technical experience rather than arbitrary skill sets. These standards will be essential to national reconstruction. To achieve this, our party will establish a National Teacher Corps to train instructors and professors to consistent standards and deploy them in underserved areas.

A nation’s strength depends on its ability to prepare its own citizens for work and civic participation. Adults with low literacy are far less likely to secure stable, living wage employment. To restore competence and eliminate educational inequity nationwide, the Phoenix Coalition will repurpose existing facilities into nationwide educational facilities. These facilities will offer apprenticeships, technical certifications, and community service programs. A centralized monitoring system will track literacy rates and workplace readiness and ensure that resources are distributed evenly throughout. Although education is a right, citizens are expected to participate in and complete this program, and the state will guarantee access to learning materials and meaningful education. The Phoenix Coalition stands against the inequity of knowledge, both the government’s weaponization of it and its neglect of those who desperately need it. The Coalition will induce a populace that is both informed about current issues and able to act on that knowledge practically. We will ensure that citizens can meaningfully contribute to our nation, think for themselves, and protect their freedom. Reform in education is what will turn our divided, struggling society into a unified, advancing one.

Second Amendment Rights

The national debate on the Second Amendment has grown increasingly polarized, with states adopting widely different gun laws and inconsistent enforcement practices. The United States continues to face elevated levels of gun violence, with more than 48,000 firearm deaths recorded in 2022 according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention). At the same time, tens of millions of Americans legally own firearms for self-defense, hunting, and sport. The current policy landscape reflects tension between the constitutional right to bear arms and the need for public safety in a society where access to weapons varies greatly across jurisdictions.

The Phoenix Coalition affirms that the right to keep and bear arms is a constitutional liberty. This right must not depend on shifting regional preferences or inconsistent state regulations. At the same time, rights carry obligations. Without responsible ownership and reliable enforcement mechanisms, individual liberty can produce instability. The Coalition rejects bans that target responsible firearm owners, but also rejects permissive systems that fail to screen individuals who pose demonstrable risks to public safety. Order arises not from prohibition or abandonment, but from structured, evidence-based regulation.

The Phoenix Coalition supports a national standard for universal background checks coordinated through updated federal databases. The Department of Justice has identified persistent gaps in the National Instant Criminal Background Check System due to incomplete or outdated records, which weaken enforcement and allow avoidable loopholes (U.S. Department of Justice). Updating these systems is necessary for accuracy and consistency.

The Coalition also supports mandatory safe-storage requirements to reduce unauthorized access and prevent accidental deaths. Additionally, the party endorses firearm licensing systems that include safety and handling training. Research from the Johns Hopkins Center for Gun Violence Solutions indicates that states with licensing and training requirements experience significantly lower firearm homicide rates, demonstrating that structured training improves safety outcomes (Johns Hopkins Center for Gun Violence Solutions).

The Coalition supports targeted enforcement measures against trafficking networks, ghost gun manufacturing, and individuals who repeatedly violate gun laws. Red flag laws that include clear due-process protections can prevent imminent harm while maintaining constitutional standards. The Coalition opposes sweeping bans on common civilian firearms, but supports regulating weapons that lack clear civilian purpose and pose heightened public risks. All policies must be transparent, data-driven, and enforced consistently to avoid arbitrary restrictions.

The Phoenix Coalition views the Second Amendment as an essential safeguard of individual liberty. To preserve that liberty, the nation must maintain structures that prevent violence and promote responsible ownership. Balanced policy, grounded in research and accountability, protects both constitutional rights and community safety. Through national standards, evidence-based regulation, and focused enforcement, the Coalition supports an approach that values freedom and order equally.
